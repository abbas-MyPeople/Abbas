# 1. The technical evolution

## Phase I: digitizing the guest interface — 2015–2019

Barceló began by creating digital guest-facing layers: direct online booking, my Barceló loyalty, mobile guest services, hotel information and ancillary reservations.

It has worked with STAY since 2016. By 2021, STAY reported implementation in 102 Barceló hotels, 438,000 unique users and 203,000 transactions. Guests could request room service, spa treatments, restaurant reservations and other services digitally. ([stay-app.com](https://www.stay-app.com/customers/barcelo-hotel-group))

This phase solved the **interface problem**:

- Give guests a digital channel.
- Reduce waiting and physical contact.
- Sell additional services.
- Capture previously invisible interactions.

But it did not yet solve the deeper problem: **who is this guest across every system, hotel, agency and trip?**

---

## Phase II: taking ownership of customer data

Barceló previously had a highly siloed technology stack, poor data governance and significant dependence on marketing agencies that controlled parts of its data and advertising infrastructure.

It adopted Tealium iQ and AudienceStream to regain control of customer data, move from stale survey-based segments to behavioral pattern-based segments, and connect customer behavior with marketing activity.

The reported results were substantial:

- Revenue increased **37% over two years**.
- Marketing costs fell by almost **6%**.
- A pattern-based campaign generated a **55% open rate**, **22% click-through rate**, **22% conversion rate** and **3× the revenue** of earlier segmentation approaches. ([tealium.com](https://tealium.com/resource/case-study/barcelo-data-transformation-results-increased-revenue-decreased-marketing-costs/))

This is important because it proves that Barceló is capable of:

1. Establishing data ownership.
2. Running controlled experiments.
3. Converting better data into measurable revenue.

It also reveals the historical weakness: Barceló’s information was not originally governed as a corporate asset.

## Phase III: turning employee memory into institutional memory — 2020–2022

In 2020, Barceló began implementing Salesforce Service Cloud under the name **BEA — Barceló Experience Assistant**. It was designed to consolidate guest knowledge that previously existed inside the memories of individual employees.

The 2020 report described BEA as a CRM capable of helping employees know and recognize guests and personalize their experience. The same period introduced the “Barceló Seamless Hotel” concept, connecting physical and digital hotel interactions through the app. ([barcelogrupo.com](https://www.barcelogrupo.com/content/dam/barcelo-group/comunicaci%C3%B3n/memorias-anuales-pdf/2020_memoria%20anual.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

By 2022, BEA had reached **80% of the company’s hotels**. It stored preferences such as:

- Preferred room.
- Whether the guest uses the spa.
- Solo versus family travel.
- Preferred restaurant.
- Previous suggestions and complaints.

Barceló was also using AI and analytics to process more than **1.2 million guest mentions and comments annually**. ([barcelogrupo.com](https://www.barcelogrupo.com/content/dam/barcelo-group/comunicaci%C3%B3n/annual-report/2022_Annual_Report_Barcelo_Web_EN_120623.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This was a profound evolution:

> Barceló was converting tacit human knowledge into queryable, portable institutional data.

That is one of its most important technical achievements.

# Barceló Hotel Group, from first principles

The previous analysis was still too technology-led. The correct starting point is not “What software does Barceló use?” It is:

> **What fundamental uncertainties and coordination failures must a hotel group eliminate to create value?**

A hotel does not manufacture inventory that can be stored. A room night expires every midnight. Service is produced and consumed at the same moment, by distributed human teams, inside physical assets that degrade continuously. Demand changes by hour, nationality, channel, weather, flight connectivity, geopolitical events and local competition.

Technology therefore has only six fundamental jobs:

1. **Know reality:** What is happening now?
2. **Predict:** What will probably happen next?
3. **Decide:** What action has the highest expected value?
4. **Coordinate:** Who or what must act?
5. **Verify:** Did the action physically happen?
6. **Learn:** Did it improve revenue, cost, satisfaction or risk?

Everything else—AI, CRM, apps, cloud, dashboards—is machinery serving one of these six functions.

My revised conclusion is:

> **Barceló is highly developed in knowing, predicting and deciding on the commercial side, but considerably less developed in coordinating, verifying and learning across the complete physical operation and the wider Grupo Barceló travel ecosystem.**

Its technology maturity is not low. It is **asymmetrical**.

# 1. The real system Barceló is trying to control

Barceló Hotel Group reported 286 hotels, 63,096 rooms and operations in 32 countries for 2025. It added 33 hotels during the year. Grupo Barceló reported €7.87 billion in revenue, €676.7 million in EBITDA and €313.4 million in profit. The group also had more than 38,600 employees. ([barcelogrupo.com](https://www.barcelogrupo.com/content/dam/barcelo-group/comunicaci%C3%B3n/annual-report/EXECUTIVE%20SUMMARY%202025.pdf))

That scale produces four fundamental forms of complexity.

## 1.1 Perishable inventory

A room that remains empty tonight can never be sold tomorrow.

The primary optimization problem is therefore not simply occupancy. It is:

\[
\text{Expected total value}
=
\text{room revenue}
+
\text{ancillary revenue}
+
\text{future customer value}
-
\text{service cost}
-
\text{channel cost}
-
\text{risk}
\]

Barceló’s executives now explicitly describe the business as optimizing the **total revenue of the hotel**, not merely selling rooms. This includes monetizing the full physical space and additional services. ([hosteltur.com](https://www.hosteltur.com/175821_ia-canal-digital-y-expansion-selectiva-las-prioridades-de-barcelo-en-un-escenario-desafiante.html))

That is conceptually correct and relatively mature.

## 1.2 Distributed execution

A centralized pricing system can change a rate almost instantly.

But if a guest requests towels, reports a broken air conditioner or reserves a restaurant, value is only created when a person inside the hotel performs the action correctly.

This distinction is fundamental:

- **Digital decision latency** can be milliseconds.
- **Physical execution latency** may be minutes or hours.
- **Verification** is often weak or manual.
- **Quality** varies by property, shift, employee and workload.

Barceló’s recent EISI implementation matters because it begins connecting digital guest requests and CRM information to housekeeping, maintenance and operational teams. But the public rollout announced in 2025 covered more than 40 vacation hotels—significant, but still only part of the portfolio. ([eisihotel.com](https://eisihotel.com/es/novedades/barcelo-hotel-group-apuesta-por-la-innovacion-de-eisi-hotel))

## 1.3 Heterogeneous control rights

Barceló does not operate every property under the same economic and legal model. Its 2024 portfolio was approximately:

- 39% managed.
- 31% owned.
- 29% leased.
- 1% franchised.

This means Barceló’s ability to impose technology, fund sensors, change processes, access data or redesign physical infrastructure differs from hotel to hotel. ([barcelogrupo.com](https://www.barcelogrupo.com/content/dam/barcelo-group/comunicaci%C3%B3n/annual-report/RE_B24_ENG_dg.pdf))

This is not a minor implementation detail. It is one of the principal reasons hotel groups struggle to achieve genuinely unified technology.

In an owned hotel, Barceló may control capital expenditure, operating procedures and system architecture.

In a managed hotel, it may control operations but depend on an owner for major investments.

In a franchise model, its control may be primarily contractual and brand-based.

Therefore, “roll out one platform everywhere” is not merely a technical problem. It is a **governance and incentive problem**.

## 1.4 Two distinct businesses inside one group

Grupo Barceló contains at least two large economic systems:

1. **Barceló Hotel Group:** accommodation, hospitality, hotel operations and destination experiences.
2. **Ávoris Corporación Empresarial:** retail travel agencies, tour operation, airline activity, incoming destination services and other travel businesses.

The group describes itself as participating in all major links of the tourism value chain. Ávoris reported 39 commercial brands, 1,645 points of sale and 6.3 million passengers in 2025. ([barcelogrupo.com](https://www.barcelogrupo.com/en/))

This should theoretically create an extraordinary data advantage.

A single group can potentially observe:

- Travel inspiration.
- Agency consultation.
- Flight or package purchase.
- Hotel reservation.
- Arrival and transfer.
- On-property behavior.
- Restaurant and spa consumption.
- Excursions.
- Complaints.
- Post-trip feedback.
- Future travel intent.

Yet I found no public evidence of a single, group-wide traveler identity, loyalty currency or decisioning platform spanning Barceló Hotel Group and Ávoris.

That does not prove it does not exist internally. But the published systems and programs remain largely divisional.

This is probably Barceló’s largest unused structural advantage.

# 5. The deepest gaps

## Gap 1: optimization horizon is too short for foundational technology

Barceló requires AI initiatives to produce return in three to six months. ([hosteltur.com](https://www.hosteltur.com/175821_ia-canal-digital-y-expansion-selectiva-las-prioridades-de-barcelo-en-un-escenario-desafiante.html))

That is excellent for filtering weak experiments.

But it creates a mathematical selection bias.

Projects capable of proving value in three to six months are usually:

- Marketing optimization.
- Content generation.
- Upselling.
- Contact-center automation.
- Forecast improvement.
- Small process automation.

Projects that fundamentally change the company often require several years:

- Global identity resolution.
- Event-driven architecture.
- Master data management.
- Cross-divisional loyalty.
- Operational digital twins.
- Enterprise semantic layers.
- Property-system modernization.

Therefore:

> Barceló’s AI discipline may systematically select incremental intelligence over foundational intelligence.

This does not mean abandoning short-term ROI. It means recognizing two different investment classes:

\[
\text{Use-case ROI}
\neq
\text{platform-option value}
\]

A shared identity platform may not produce a clear three-month return, but it enables dozens of later use cases.

**Strategic gravity: 9/10**

---

## Gap 2: customer profile is not the same as customer identity

Barceló says its CRM integrates multiple sources, preferences, stay history and reservation type. ([hosteltur.com](https://www.hosteltur.com/175821_ia-canal-digital-y-expansion-selectiva-las-prioridades-de-barcelo-en-un-escenario-desafiante.html))

That is valuable, but identity has stricter requirements.

A true enterprise identity system must decide whether these are the same person:

- A traveler booking through Barcelo.com.
- A spouse listed on another reservation.
- An Ávoris agency customer.
- An Iberojet passenger.
- A conference attendee.
- A restaurant customer.
- A spa customer.
- A guest using a different email address.
- A customer who booked through an OTA with masked contact information.

CRM records can contain rich profiles while still producing duplicates and incomplete cross-channel recognition.

The public loyalty architecture remains centered on direct Barceló hotel bookings. I found no public evidence of a unified identity spanning the hotel and travel divisions. ([barcelo.com](https://www.barcelo.com/en-us/mybarcelo/))

**Strategic gravity: 10/10**

---

## Gap 3: no visible end-to-end owner of the guest-to-operation loop

Consider one simple event:

> A high-value returning guest reports that the air conditioning is not working.

The chain may involve:

1. Barceló App or contact center.
2. BEA/Salesforce.
3. Customer Experience.
4. EISI.
5. Maintenance.
6. Hotel management.
7. Compensation decision.
8. CRM update.
9. Future personalization.
10. Revenue or retention analysis.

Organizational ownership is divided among:

- Digital Business.
- Customer Experience.
- IT.
- SER Barceló.
- Hotel Operations.
- Commercial.
- Local hotel management.

Each department may perform its own component correctly while the total outcome fails.

This is the classic problem of local optimization:

\[
\sum \text{successful departmental tasks}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{successful customer outcome}
\]

I found no publicly visible executive role explicitly accountable for the complete digital-to-physical service loop.

That is an inference from the reconstructed organization, not evidence of internal dysfunction.

**Strategic gravity: 9.5/10**

---

## Gap 4: real-time commercial state is ahead of real-time operational state

Barceló publicly discusses near-real-time pricing, marketing and distribution decisions.

By contrast, the operational transformation through EISI is comparatively recent and still rolling through the estate. ([hosteltur.com](https://www.hosteltur.com/175821_ia-canal-digital-y-expansion-selectiva-las-prioridades-de-barcelo-en-un-escenario-desafiante.html))

This creates a structural imbalance.

The company may know with high precision:

- Who is likely to book.
- What rate to present.
- What upgrade to offer.
- Which marketing action to activate.

But have weaker real-time knowledge of:

- Whether a room will actually be ready.
- Whether a restaurant is at capacity.
- Whether the spa can fulfill another offer.
- Whether maintenance backlog is increasing.
- Whether housekeeping is understaffed.
- Whether a promised benefit was delivered.

This produces a dangerous condition:

> The company can make increasingly precise promises without equivalent precision in fulfillment.

**Strategic gravity: 9/10**

---

## Gap 5: data governance is still being built after use cases have proliferated

The Data Office role strongly suggests Barceló is now formalizing domain ownership, stewardship, quality and lineage after years of platform expansion. ([barcelo.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com](https://barcelo.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/Barcelo_Careers/job/Responsable-de-la-Oficina-del-Dato_JR10513-1))

This is a common pattern:

1. Departments build useful systems.
2. Data proliferates.
3. Analytics expands.
4. Definitions diverge.
5. Reconciliation becomes expensive.
6. A Data Office is created.

Barceló appears somewhere between stages four and six.

The risk is not simply inaccurate dashboards. It includes:

- Different customer-value definitions.
- Inconsistent revenue attribution.
- Duplicate identities.
- Conflicting hotel and corporate reports.
- AI trained on inconsistent labels.
- Data-access friction.
- Privacy risk.
- Slow integration after acquisitions.

**Strategic gravity: 9/10**

---

## Gap 6: a collection of AI use cases is not an AI operating system

Barceló’s 40-plus use cases are evidence of active adoption. ([hosteltur.com](https://www.hosteltur.com/175821_ia-canal-digital-y-expansion-selectiva-las-prioridades-de-barcelo-en-un-escenario-desafiante.html))

But use-case count is a weak maturity metric.

Forty independent models can create:

- Forty data pipelines.
- Forty monitoring systems.
- Forty definitions.
- Forty vendor dependencies.
- Forty access-control surfaces.
- Forty separate feedback loops.

# What Rafael Palou is actually doing now

**Rafael Palou remains CIO of Grupo Barceló as of July 2026.** The clearest current evidence is Forbes España’s 2026 CIO list and Barceló’s own recent communications. His remit is described as global technology and data strategy, enterprise architecture, cybersecurity, cloud platforms and the introduction of AI into corporate systems. His current work is best understood as **six overlapping transformation programmes**, not one AI initiative.

## 1. Formalizing Barceló’s data constitution

Barceló rebuilt its corporate information platform around a lakehouse architecture by 2023. Palou publicly presented that architecture with Cívica at Snowflake’s Data Cloud World Tour. Current recruitment still explicitly references Snowflake, BI integration, ETL/ELT, data reconciliation and data-quality controls. ([ituser.es](https://www.ituser.es/actualidad/2023/10/snowflake-muestra-en-data-cloud-world-tour-como-llevar-la-ia-a-los-datos))

However, Barceló is now recruiting a **Data Governance Manager** to create and lead its corporate Data Office. The role is not a maintenance position. It is expected to:

- Define the corporate data strategy.
- Establish Data Owners and Data Stewards.
- Create a corporate glossary.
- Establish data-quality and certification standards.
- Ensure lineage, coherence and traceability.
- Coordinate access with Legal and Security.
- Report to a project committee sponsored by General Management. ([barcelo.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com](https://barcelo.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/en-US/Barcelo_Careers/job/Responsable-de-la-Oficina-del-Dato_JR10513-1))

This produces a crucial conclusion:

> **Barceló built significant data-consumption capability before fully institutionalizing data governance.**

That does not mean the lakehouse failed. It means Barceló reached the point where adding more dashboards and AI models without common definitions would create diminishing returns.

For example, the organization must establish one authoritative meaning for:

- Guest.
- Stay.
- Booking.
- Cancellation.
- Room revenue.
- Ancillary revenue.
- Direct customer.
- Complaint resolved.
- Returning customer.
- Available room.
- Out-of-service room.

Without that semantic foundation, different systems can be technically connected while still disagreeing about reality.

### What Palou is likely doing here

The publicly visible sequence is:

\[
\text{Cloud/data platform}
\rightarrow
\text{widespread analytics}
\rightarrow
\text{semantic conflicts}
\rightarrow
\text{formal Data Office}
\]

That suggests his current data priority is less about acquiring another platform and more about establishing **authority over the meaning and acceptable use of data**.

Central data, revenue management, commercial standards, CRM, cybersecurity policies, enterprise systems and common reporting.

### Property execution plane

Local hotel systems, staff, networks, workflows, contracts, physical infrastructure and varying adoption.

The primary technical challenge is keeping these two planes synchronized.

## First principle

Traditional software executes predetermined instructions.

> “AI changes a reservation, contacts a customer, creates compensation, routes a maintenance task, modifies an offer or interacts with another agent”

- Did the API work?
- Did the server respond?
- Was the user authenticated?

The company must now ask:

- Which agent acted?
- On whose authority?
- What goal was it pursuing?
- What information did it observe?
- Which tools did it call?
- What decisions did it make between calls?
- Did it violate a business rule?
- What happened when a downstream system failed?
- Did it retry, compensate, escalate or falsely claim success?
- Can the entire event be reconstructed?
- Can the company measure whether the agent created economic value or merely activity?

\[
\text{Customer requests change}

\text{agent calls reservation system}
\rightarrow
\text{system times out}
