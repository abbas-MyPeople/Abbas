# The master taxonomy — 14 families of irreducible human goods

> **Read this caveat first.** This taxonomy answers *"what can humans do that AI can't?"* — a
> defensive question that finds **refuges**. It is real but it is only half the map, and it
> systematically misses positional categories like live selling, creators as demand infrastructure,
> streamers and forward-deployed engineers. The offensive half — *"when AI makes X abundant, what
> becomes the bottleneck?"* — is in
> [`09-interface-thesis-and-generative-engines.md`](09-interface-thesis-and-generative-engines.md).
> Families 13 and 14 below were generated by that corrected method and were added after the fact.

*Companion to [`00-thesis-and-mechanisms.md`](00-thesis-and-mechanisms.md). Read that first for the
nine forces referenced throughout as F1–F9.*

---

## How this is organized (and why not by industry)

Industry is the wrong unit. "Healthcare" contains both the most automatable work in the economy
(coding, claims, triage, radiology reads) and the least (a hand on a dying person). Sorting by
industry guarantees you'll be wrong about half of it.

So this map sorts by **what the human is actually selling** — the irreducible good underneath the
job title. Twelve families. A profession can appear in several; that's a feature, and the ones
appearing in three or more are the most defensible things in the economy.

Within each family, four tiers:

- **T1 — Obvious / already booming.** Visible today, evidence in [`02-evidence-ledger.md`](02-evidence-ledger.md).
- **T2 — Evolution in flight.** Exists today, mutating into a different shape right now. *This is
  where most of the value is, and it's the tier people skip.*
- **T3 — Non-obvious / emerging.** Early signal, mostly unnamed, 1–4 years out.
- **T4 — Speculative.** Plausible from the forces, no real evidence yet. Flagged honestly as such.

**Force stack** = which of F1–F9 support the family. **Kill risk** = what falsifies it.

The forces, in shorthand: F1 Baumol/scarcity inversion · F2 verification · F3 liability sink ·
F4 embodiment gap · F5 belonging deficit · F6 demographics · F7 status/ritual · F8 authenticity ·
F9 presence.

## Family 1 — PRESENCE
### *Selling: being in the same room while something unrepeatable happens*

**Force stack:** F9, F7, F5, F1 (4) · **Maturity:** booming now · **Kill risk:** affordability ceiling

The flagship category and the one everyone names first. Worth stating precisely *why* it works:
a recording is infinitely reproducible and therefore deflating toward zero; the night itself is
produced exactly once, and its supply is capped by physical seats. Recorded music went to ~$0.003
a stream while the concert went to $150+. Same artist, same songs. **Only the reproducibility
changed.**

**T1 — Obvious**
- Live music: concerts, festivals, residencies. Live Nation cleared $25.2B revenue and 159M fans in
  2025, with 2026 pacing up double digits and >80% of large-venue shows already booked by February.
- Live comedy — arena-scale stand-up, comics graduating from clubs to 1,500–3,000-seat theaters.
- Live sport as spectacle; theater; museums and immersive attractions (Sphere residencies, Meow Wolf
  expanding to LA and NYC).
- Conferences, conventions, fan gatherings.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Venue-as-format, not venue-as-container.** Sphere is not a big room, it's a medium. Expect
  purpose-built formats where the building *is* the IP, and content licensed into it.
- **Residency over touring.** Economically superior (fixed production, no freight, destination
  demand). Watch mid-tier artists adopt what Vegas normalized.
- **Event cinema.** Film released as an event with a window and a crowd, not a catalog item.
- **Creator IRL.** Podcast live tours, YouTuber meetups, newsletter dinners — the migration of
  parasocial audiences into paid physical rooms. The highest-margin move available to any creator,
  and structurally the same trade as the concert.
- **Phone-free as a product tier.** Q1 2026 phone-free event volume was already >⅓ of all of 2025.
  Yondr-style enforcement moved from artist quirk to bookable feature.
- **Participatory over spectator.** Hyrox, marathons, pickleball, obstacle racing. The customer
  stops buying a seat and buys *a role in the event*. Higher price, higher retention, and it
  converts a spectator business into a training-and-community subscription.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Presence certification / "verified live."** As AI video makes "was this actually live?"
  unanswerable, expect cryptographic attestation of liveness — C2PA-style provenance applied to
  events. A boring compliance product hiding a large moat.
- **Scarcity-engineered experience.** Deliberately unrecordable, unrepeatable, unstreamable events
  sold at a premium *because* they leave no artifact. The anti-content business model.
- **Small-room premium inversion.** As arenas price up and out, expect a barbell: 50-seat rooms
  priced *above* 5,000-seat rooms per head, on intimacy rather than spectacle. Already visible in
  dining (chef's counters) before it hits music.
- **Local/civic presence.** Town halls, community assemblies, local journalism as live events —
  rebuilt as businesses once national digital media finishes collapsing into slop.
- **Corporate presence.** Offsites are a ~$73.7B industry *because of* remote work, not despite it.
  The offsite is the enterprise version of the concert ticket.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Presence brokerage: markets for "a person will physically attend on your behalf" (already exists
  in queueing and line-sitting; formalizes into proxy attendance, inspection, witnessing).
- Anti-broadcast venues: legally binding no-capture spaces as a licensed category.
- Post-remote-work re-agglomeration: if verification (F2) keeps failing remotely, physical
  co-location becomes a security control and cities re-price accordingly.

## Family 2 — TOUCH
### *Selling: hands on a human body*

**Force stack:** F4, F6, F1, F5 (4) · **Maturity:** structurally growing · **Kill risk:** robotics after ~2032; wage suppression

The most physically irreducible family, and the worst-compensated. Any honest version of this thesis
has to hold both facts at once.

**T1 — Obvious**
- Nursing, home health and personal care aides (largest absolute job growth of any US occupation,
  ~739,800 added through 2034), physical/occupational therapy, massage, chiropractic, dental
  hygiene, aesthetics/med-spa, barbering and hair.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Hands + AI brain.** The clinician's diagnostic cognition gets augmented or replaced; the value
  concentrates in examination, procedure, and the human relationship. The job doesn't die, it
  *rebalances* — and the rebalanced version is harder to train for, not easier.
- **Birth and death at the ends.** Doulas at both ends of life. Death doulas grew from ~260 to
  1,600+ NEDA members between 2019 and 2024. This is a T1-becoming-T2 category riding straight into
  the demographic wave.
- **Aesthetic medicine as recurring service.** Already the fastest-consumerizing corner of medicine;
  benefits from both F7 (status) and F4 (embodiment).
- **Home as the site of care.** Care migrating out of institutions into houses — which converts a
  facilities business into a logistics-and-trust business, and makes the *worker* the brand.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Touch as a standalone prescribed good.** Somatic therapy, contact/co-regulation practices,
  therapeutic touch decoupled from massage's spa framing and reframed as nervous-system healthcare.
  Currently fringe-coded; the loneliness data (F5) is the leading indicator.
- **Robot-assisted, human-fronted care.** The lift, the transfer, the medication is mechanized; the
  human keeps the face and the responsibility. This is the *most likely* shape of care in 2032, and
  it's a job-quality question, not a job-existence question.
- **Care coordination as a paid profession for families.** Someone must orchestrate the aide, the
  specialist, the insurer, the AI triage bot. Adult children are doing this for free right now at
  enormous cost to their careers; it will professionalize. (See also Family 11.)
- **Longevity concierge.** The measurement is automated; the protocol adherence needs a human
  standing in front of you. That's a coach, not a doctor.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Licensed platonic-touch practitioners as an insured, credentialed category.
- Sensory-deprivation-driven demand: as more of life is screen-mediated, high-touch experiences
  price as luxury rather than health.

## Family 3 — HANDS ON THE WORLD
### *Selling: embodied skill applied to physical matter*

**Force stack:** F4, F1, F7, F8 (4) · **Maturity:** booming now · **Kill risk:** humanoid robotics; immigration/labor policy

**T1 — Obvious**
- Electricians, HVAC, plumbing, welding, industrial mechanics, linemen. The AI buildout is the
  demand driver: 300,000+ new electricians needed for data centers alone; data-center specialists
  reported well into six figures; ~60% of surveyed Gen Z say they'll pursue trade work.
- Construction, machining, field service, agriculture, marine.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **The trades acquire status.** This is a *status boom* (per §5 of the thesis doc) more than a
  price boom — and status booms reshape talent pipelines for a generation. The "toolbelt generation"
  framing is marketing, but the enrollment numbers under it are real.
- **Trade + software operator.** The person who runs the robot, the drone, the diagnostic rig. Pay
  premium goes to the hybrid, and the hybrid is trained nowhere.
- **Repair economy.** Right-to-repair plus goods deflation plus authenticity (F8) makes fixing
  things a viable premium service again rather than a cost-of-last-resort.
- **Craft as luxury.** Ceramics, textiles, furniture, tailoring, bespoke everything. Directly rides
  the human-made premium (68% preference at price parity).

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Physical-world verification.** Someone must physically confirm the thing exists: inspectors,
  appraisers, auditors of physical assets, insurance adjusters. As remote documentation becomes
  forgeable (F2), *going and looking* becomes the control. Massively underrated category.
- **Robot wrangler / fleet field tech.** Every deployed robot fleet needs humans for exceptions,
  recovery, maintenance, and the last 5% of dexterity. The unglamorous job that scales *with*
  automation rather than against it.
- **Decommissioning and remediation.** Physical undoing of the last era — retrofit, teardown,
  environmental cleanup, grid rework. Enormous, boring, unstoppable.
- **Custody of physical originals.** Archives, vaults, sample libraries, physical masters. When
  digital provenance is contestable, the physical original becomes the ground truth.

**T4 — Speculative**
- "Analog assurance" — deliberately non-networked physical systems (paper ballots, mechanical
  backups, air-gapped records) as a service, staffed by people, sold on unhackability.
- Grid, water and food-system labor repriced upward as strategic rather than menial.

## Family 4 — JUDGMENT UNDER ACCOUNTABILITY
### *Selling: a person who signs, and who can be sued*

**Force stack:** F3, F2, F1 (3, but very deep) · **Maturity:** forming now · **Kill risk:** regulatory reversal

The single most underrated family, because it looks like a bureaucratic artifact and is actually a
structural moat. The good being sold is not expertise — the model may well have more. **The good is
consequence-bearing.**

**T1 — Obvious**
- Physicians, attorneys, PEs, CPAs, actuaries, licensed architects, fiduciaries, notaries.
  Licensure keeps a human on the signature line.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **The professional as underwriter.** Work product is machine-generated; the professional's job
  becomes review, judgment and *assumption of liability*. Fewer people, far more leverage each,
  priced on risk borne rather than hours worked. **Watch billing models shift from time to risk —
  that's the tell that a profession has completed this transition.**
- **Compliance-mandated oversight roles.** EU AI Act Art. 14 / NIST RMF create a genuinely new job:
  the trained, provable human overseer. Currently being filled badly by people without authority to
  overrule — which is the failure mode, and therefore the product opportunity.
- **Expert witness inflation.** When documentary evidence is forgeable, live human testimony gains
  weight. Courts are the last institution that fundamentally trusts a body in a room under oath.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **The liability sponge as a business model.** Firms whose actual product is "we will be
  accountable for your AI's output" — insured, credentialed, priced on exposure. Early forms exist
  in audit and medical over-read; expect it as a horizontal category.
- **AI incident forensics.** Reconstructing what an autonomous system did and why, for courts and
  regulators. Half engineer, half investigator, and adversarial by nature — so it can't be run by
  the same AI.
- **Model/agent auditors with personal liability.** The financial-audit structure ported to AI
  systems: a named partner signs the opinion.
- **In-person diligence.** Deals, hires and closings pushed back into rooms because remote identity
  can't be trusted. Quiet reversal of a decade of remote-first process.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Personal liability licensing for AI deployment — a "responsible operator" credential, held by a
  human, required to run consequential agents.
- Insurance markets pricing "human-signed" vs "machine-signed" work differently, which would
  formalize the entire family into a measurable spread.

## Family 5 — BELONGING
### *Selling: membership in a group that would not exist without you*

**Force stack:** F5, F9, F7 (3) · **Maturity:** booming now · **Kill risk:** AI companionship substitution; commoditization

**T1 — Obvious**
- Fitness communities, run clubs, boutique studios, wellness third-spaces (bathhouses, sauna clubs),
  private membership clubs, religious congregations (Gen Z attendance rising notably among young
  men), recovery and peer-support groups.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Matchmaking replaces matching.** Dating-app fatigue is pushing demand toward human matchmakers,
  curated events and introductions. *Note: some circulating 2026 numbers on app decline are poorly
  sourced — see the evidence ledger. The direction is well-supported; the magnitude is not.*
- **Commercial third places.** The bar/church/bowling-league functions are being rebuilt as
  membership businesses with a P&L. The economics are subscription; the product is other people.
- **Host as a paid profession.** Supper clubs, dinner-party companies, community curators. The
  skill — assembling a room of strangers who click — is real, teachable, and now purchasable.
- **Youth sports as social infrastructure.** ~$40B and growing hard, with families taking on debt
  to participate. It is not really about athletics; it's purchased community and status for parents
  as much as kids.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Belonging as an employee benefit.** Once loneliness is a measurable health cost, employers buy
  community the way they buy gym memberships. HR budget, not consumer budget — which is a 10x price
  change for the same product.
- **Secular congregations.** Weekly, obligatory, multi-generational, with ritual and mutual
  obligation but no metaphysics. Repeatedly attempted and mostly failed (Sunday Assembly);
  conditions are now far more favorable.
- **Anti-algorithmic social.** Groups whose explicit promise is that no algorithm selected the
  people. The Offline Club and similar are the consumer wedge; the B2B version is recruiting.
- **Loneliness-outcome contracting.** Payers (insurers, Medicare Advantage) reimbursing social
  connection because isolation drives cost. The moment a CPT code exists for this, the category
  changes scale overnight. **This is one of the highest-leverage things to watch in the whole map.**
- **Intergenerational brokerage.** Deliberately mixing age cohorts — the demographic the aging wave
  and the loneliness wave both need, and nobody serves.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Belonging insurance / prescribed community as covered benefit.
- Residential design sold on social outcomes rather than square footage (co-living's serious sequel).

## Family 6 — TRANSFORMATION
### *Selling: making you into a different person*

**Force stack:** F1, F7, F5 (3) · **Maturity:** mid-transition · **Kill risk:** AI tutors proving genuinely superior

The distinction that makes this family durable: **information transfer is fully automatable;
behavior change mostly isn't.** People do not fail to get fit, sober, educated or promoted because
they lack information. The human is there to supply accountability, belief, and social pressure —
none of which a system you can close the tab on has ever supplied well.

**T1 — Obvious**
- Personal trainers, executive coaches, therapists (137M Americans live in a mental-health shortage
  area; demand projected to outpace supply ~4.5:1), tutors, music/art instructors, physical rehab.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Teacher → guide/coach.** Alpha School's model — AI delivers academics, adult "guides" handle
  motivation, character and life skills — is the clearest live experiment. Note honestly that
  research does *not* yet show AI tutors beating human teachers; the model is unproven on outcomes
  even as it spreads.
- **Therapy unbundles.** AI absorbs the low-acuity, always-on layer; humans concentrate on high
  acuity, trauma, group work and anything requiring embodied co-regulation. Group therapy and peer
  support specialists scale where 1:1 can't.
- **Apprenticeship revives.** As credentials deflate (anyone can produce credential-shaped output),
  demonstrated capability under a named master reflates. The oldest training model returns because
  it's the one AI can't fake.
- **Coaching swallows adjacent professions.** Financial advisor → behavioral coach with fiduciary
  duty. Nutritionist → adherence coach. Recruiter → career coach. In each case the analytical core
  automates and the *relationship* becomes the product.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Re-skilling as continuous, human-led infrastructure.** If AI churns job categories faster than
  institutions retrain, the retrainer is a permanent role, not a bootcamp.
- **Judgment and taste training.** Teaching people to evaluate machine output — the single most
  valuable transferable skill of the next decade, and currently taught essentially nowhere.
- **Attention rehabilitation.** Treating fractured attention as a trainable deficit. Currently sold
  as digital-detox retreats; will medicalize.
- **Character and virtue as explicit curriculum.** Once knowledge is free, parents pay for
  formation. This is already the quiet actual pitch of expensive schooling; it will become the
  explicit one.
- **Meaning/vocation counseling for displaced professionals.** Not career services — closer to
  chaplaincy for people whose identity was their expertise. Underserved and about to be enormous.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Licensed "human development" practitioners spanning today's coach/therapist/teacher split.
- Employer-funded transformation as retention: companies paying for who you become, not what you do.

## Family 7 — TASTE AND CURATION
### *Selling: choosing, when everything is infinite and free*

**Force stack:** F8, F7, F1 (3) · **Maturity:** early · **Kill risk:** personalization models genuinely solving taste

Abundance destroys the value of *access* and inflates the value of *selection*. When any restaurant,
song, product or idea is one prompt away, the bottleneck is no longer finding — it's deciding. And
taste is a trust relationship: you don't want the objectively optimal pick, you want *the pick from
someone whose judgment you've decided to adopt.*

**T1 — Obvious**
- Sommeliers, stylists, interior designers, buyers, editors, A&R, gallerists, chefs as tastemakers,
  personal shoppers, clienteling in luxury retail.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Curator as the brand.** Individual humans out-earning the institutions they left, because trust
  attaches to a person and not a logo. Already true in media; spreading to retail, travel, finance.
- **Scouting reflates.** When output is cheap and undifferentiated, the ability to spot the real
  thing early gets more valuable, not less. Talent, music, literature, venture.
- **Curated commerce over search.** As search results fill with generated content, human-picked
  inventory becomes the discovery channel. The "shop" replaces the "search."

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Anti-slop curation as a paid utility.** Somebody has to certify "a human read this and it's
  worth your time." Newsletters are the primitive form; the mature form is a paid filtering layer
  across whole categories.
- **Provenance-aware curation.** Curators who guarantee *how* something was made, not just that it's
  good. Merges Family 7 with Family 9.
- **Taste as licensed IP.** Individuals licensing their preference function — the model is trained
  on you, and you get paid for it. Blurs directly into Family 12.
- **Local human recommendation networks.** As AI recommendation homogenizes globally, the
  neighbor-who-knows becomes the differentiated channel. Directly relevant to local businesses —
  see [`05-implications-and-plays.md`](05-implications-and-plays.md).

**T4 — Speculative**
- Curation guilds with enforceable standards and disclosure of AI use.
- "Taste audits" for brands whose output has drifted into generated sameness.

## Family 8 — WITNESS AND RITUAL
### *Selling: certifying that a thing happened, and marking it*

**Force stack:** F2, F7, F9, F3 (4) · **Maturity:** early · **Kill risk:** none obvious — this is the most durable family

The most anthropologically deep family and the least commercially mapped. Humans need other humans
to *witness* transitions — birth, marriage, death, initiation, achievement — and a witness that
isn't a person doesn't discharge the need. No amount of model capability changes this, because the
value is definitionally in who observed it.

**T1 — Obvious**
- Clergy, wedding officiants, funeral directors, celebrants, notaries, referees and umpires,
  proctors, chaplains (hospital, military, corporate).

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Ritual decouples from religion.** Celebration-of-life services, personalized funerals, secular
  celebrants, living funerals, death cafés. The *ritual* demand survives the institutional decline
  and gets re-provisioned commercially.
- **Grief and end-of-life as a services market** (grief support ~$4B in 2024, projected to roughly
  double by 2032), riding the demographic wave head-on.
- **Proctoring returns.** Remote assessment is now trivially defeatable; in-person invigilation
  reflates. A dead industry resurrected by AI, which is a pattern worth naming.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Secular rites of passage.** Graduations, career transitions, divorces, recovery milestones,
  citizenship, menopause, retirement — currently unmarked or badly marked. A designed-ritual
  profession is a genuinely open category.
- **Human witness as a legal/financial primitive.** In-person attestation for high-value
  transactions because remote identity failed. Notary, but expanded and repriced.
- **Achievement verification.** When any credential can be generated, the person who watched you do
  it becomes the credential. Ports directly to hiring: the reference, revived as an industry.
- **Corporate ritual design.** Onboarding, milestones and departures as designed ceremony —
  the retention lever remote work removed and nobody replaced.
- **AI-era mourning.** Ritual around losses that didn't previously exist: obsoleted professions,
  digital afterlives, relationships with discontinued models. Sounds absurd; the demand is already
  visible in how people react to model deprecations.

**T4 — Speculative**
- A licensed civil-celebrant profession spanning all secular transitions.
- Witness networks as infrastructure — an on-call human attestation layer, the physical-world
  analogue of a certificate authority.

## Family 9 — TRUST AND VERIFICATION
### *Selling: proof that a human was here*

**Force stack:** F2, F3, F7 (3) · **Maturity:** exploding now · **Kill risk:** a technical solution that removes the human

The fastest-moving family in the map and the one least predicted three years ago. It is also
philosophically strange: these are jobs whose entire content is *establishing humanness*, which only
makes sense in a world where that is in doubt.

**T1 — Obvious**
- Identity verification, KYC, background checks, fraud investigation, security guards, executive
  protection.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Proof-of-personhood as infrastructure.** World's $52.5M raise, World ID in Zoom/DocuSign/Okta/
  Tinder, Zoom's "Verified Human" badges (April 2026). With a reported 64%+ of web traffic
  non-human, this stops being crypto-adjacent and becomes plumbing.
- **Content provenance (C2PA)** moving from optional to mandatory in professional capture.
- **Anti-deepfake process design.** Post-Arup ($25M lost to a fully synthetic video call), the
  defense is procedural, not perceptual: out-of-band callbacks, physical confirmation, human
  escalation paths. Somebody has to design, staff and audit those.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **In-person as a security control.** The most important second-order effect in this family:
  when remote channels can't be trusted, physical presence becomes a *compliance requirement*, not a
  preference. This quietly reprices offices, travel, closings, onboarding and executive meetings —
  and it cuts directly against a decade of remote-first assumption.
- **Human attestation services.** On-demand "a verified human physically confirmed X." Inspection,
  identity, condition of goods, proof of life for pensions.
- **Relationship-based trust as fraud control.** The oldest technology: you can't deepfake the
  banker who's known you fifteen years. Small institutions have an unexpected advantage here.
- **"Certified human" as a labeling economy.** Badges, unions, guilds and audit bodies for AI-free
  work. Note Capgemini's finding that the label adds little *today* — because human is still the
  default assumption. The label's value switches on the day that assumption flips.
- **Executive protection expands to identity.** Protecting the principal's *likeness and voice* as
  well as their body.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Personhood credentials as a prerequisite for economic participation (with real civil-liberties
  problems — worth flagging, not celebrating).
- Human-only marketplaces, verified end-to-end, priced at a premium.

## Family 10 — CARE
### *Selling: sustained responsibility for someone who cannot manage alone*

**Force stack:** F6, F4, F1, F3 (4) · **Maturity:** structural, unstoppable · **Kill risk:** wage suppression makes it a bad job, not a small one

Distinguished from TOUCH (physical) and BELONGING (social) because the good is *continuity of
responsibility over time*. The largest family by headcount and the one where "goes bananas" is most
misleading — it will boom in volume and may not boom in prosperity.

**T1 — Obvious**
- Home health aides, elder care, childcare, special needs support, hospice, disability services,
  veterinary care.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Care coordination professionalizes.** The orchestration layer between family, aides,
  specialists, insurers and AI triage. Currently unpaid labor performed by daughters.
- **Aging in place.** Sensors and models handle monitoring; humans handle everything monitoring
  detects. Automation *creates* care visits by surfacing needs earlier.
- **Care as an employer benefit** — the elder-care equivalent of childcare benefits, driven by
  workforce retention among the sandwich generation.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Care for the caregivers.** Respite as a product. ~53M unpaid US caregivers is a market nobody
  serves because the customer is exhausted and broke — which means the buyer has to be an employer
  or a payer, not the caregiver.
- **Pet care premiumization** as fertility falls and pets absorb care instinct and budget.
- **Fertility decline inverts the pyramid**: fewer children, more elders, fewer family caregivers
  per elder. The gap is filled commercially or not at all. This is arithmetic, not forecasting.
- **Chosen-family infrastructure.** Legal, financial and logistical structures for people aging
  without children — a demographic about to become historically unprecedented in size.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Care cooperatives / worker-owned models as the response to wage suppression.
- Migration policy as the dominant variable in care economics — arguably it already is.

## Family 11 — ADVOCACY AND REDRESS
### *Selling: a human who fights the machine on your behalf*

**Force stack:** F3, F5, F1 (3) · **Maturity:** early, high-conviction · **Kill risk:** regulation mandating free redress

**The single most under-mapped family in this document**, and the one I'd bet on hardest for
net-new company formation. As consequential decisions get automated — claims, credit, hiring,
moderation, pricing, benefits — the number of people wronged by a system with no one to appeal to
grows monotonically. Every automated decision layer generates its own demand for human intercession.
This is a *structural byproduct* of automation, not a reaction to it.

**T1 — Obvious**
- Patient advocates, insurance claims appeals, special-education advocates, ombudsmen, union reps,
  consumer attorneys, tenant advocates.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Denial-fighting as a category.** Automated claim denial at scale has already created automated
  and human appeal services; the human tier is where the hard cases and the fees are.
- **Algorithmic-harm practice areas** in law: hiring discrimination, credit, pricing, content
  removal.
- **Escalation-to-human as the actual customer service product.** Not an overflow path — the paid
  premium tier. Consumers already expect this split; 42% say they'd pay extra for access to a human
  rep.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **The AI ombudsman.** An independent human you retain to contest automated decisions across your
  whole life — insurance, credit, platform bans, benefits, school placement. Retainer or membership.
  Nobody owns this and it should exist.
- **Account/identity recovery specialists.** Locked out by an automated system with no human on the
  other end is now a common catastrophic life event. There's no 911 for it.
- **Institutional navigators.** Healthcare, immigration, benefits, school systems — professional
  wayfinding through systems that got more automated and *less* navigable simultaneously.
- **Reputation repair against synthetic content.** Defending real people against AI-generated
  defamation and impersonation. Half legal, half technical, entirely human-fronted.
- **Model-harm case workers.** Support for people harmed by AI companionship, gambling-style
  engagement loops, or automated decisions — an adjacent category to today's addiction counseling,
  and the litigation is already starting.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Statutory right to human review, creating a mandated profession overnight (EU direction of travel
  makes this plausible, not fanciful).
- Redress insurance: a policy that buys you a human advocate when an automated system harms you.

## Family 12 — HUMAN EXPERIENCE AS INPUT
### *Selling: being human, to the machines*

**Force stack:** F1, F4, F8 (3) · **Maturity:** booming now, possibly transient · **Kill risk:** synthetic data closing the gap — the highest kill risk in the map

The category that inverts the whole thesis, and the one nobody saw coming. Not humans competing with
AI, and not humans serving humans — **humans as the scarce input to AI.** The one irreplaceable
resource in the AI supply chain is human judgment, experience and embodiment, and there's a
functioning market for it right now.

**T1 — Obvious (and already large)**
- Expert data marketplaces. Mercor: ~$760M annualized revenue at end-2025 → ~$2B by June 2026,
  raised at a $10B valuation in Oct 2025 with reports of $20B talks by July 2026, paying out
  >$1.5M/day to contractors at up to ~$200/hour. Surge reportedly out-earned Scale with $1B+
  revenue. Doctors, lawyers, bankers, PhDs renting their judgment by the hour.
- RLHF, red-teaming, evaluation, domain expert review.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Expertise unbundles from employment.** A cardiologist's judgment becomes a directly monetizable
  asset, sold by the hour to labs, independent of practicing. That is a genuinely new labor market.
- **Robot teleoperation and demonstration.** Humans paid to physically demonstrate tasks so robots
  can learn them. A skilled teleoperator produces 5–50 episodes/hour, which is the binding
  constraint on embodied AI. **Note the vicious irony: this is the best-paid new physical-labor
  category and its explicit purpose is to eliminate physical-labor categories.**
- **Egocentric life capture** — always-on human video collection for embodied AI.
- **Frontier evaluation** — humans who can still tell whether a superhuman output is correct. A
  shrinking pool, therefore a rising price.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Taste and preference supply.** Not correctness — *preference*. Which of these is better?
  Unautomatable by construction, because the target is human preference itself.
- **Experience capture as a job.** Being paid to live, notice and narrate — the ethnographic layer
  models can't obtain from text. Early forms in specialized data collection.
- **Tacit knowledge extraction.** Debriefing masters of physical crafts before they retire. A
  race against mortality with a real clock on it.
- **Culturally-situated data supply.** Language, custom, and context from communities models
  underserve. Both a real economic opportunity and a real exploitation risk.
- **Human disagreement as a product.** Where experts genuinely diverge is exactly where models
  need signal and can't manufacture it.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Individuals licensing a personal judgment model and earning royalties on it.
- Certified human-origin data as a regulated commodity with a price index.
- The bleak version: this family is a *bridge* — well-paid now, and the bridge is being paid to
  burn itself. Plan careers accordingly.

## Family 13 — PERSUASION AND TRUST TRANSFER
### *Selling: a decision made on someone else's authority*

**Force stack:** F7, F8, F9, F2 (4) · **Maturity:** booming now · **Kill risk:** agentic commerce
capturing the decision layer

*Added after the fact — see [`09-interface-thesis-and-generative-engines.md`](09-interface-thesis-and-generative-engines.md)
for why the original method missed this and why it should have been Family 1.*

The good being sold is not information — information is free and infinite. It's **the transfer of
trust from a person to a decision.** When every product has infinite reviews, infinite comparisons
and an AI that will summarize all of them, the binding constraint on a purchase is no longer
knowledge. It's *conviction*, and conviction moves between humans.

The numbers make this the best-quantified family in the entire taxonomy, because unlike survey-based
"human premium" evidence, nobody is asked anything — they just buy more:

**T1 — Obvious**
- Enterprise and complex B2B sales, executive search, fundraising, political canvassing, real estate
  negotiation, luxury clienteling, auctioneering.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Live selling.** US live commerce hit **$42B in 2026 growing 36% CAGR**; Whatnot alone did **$8B+
  live GMV** with ~60% share of a ~$22B NA+EU market; TikTok Live converts at **7.8% vs 2.1% for
  feed ads — 3.7x**. Live's share of US TikTok Shop GMV went 14% → 26% in two years. QVC, forty
  years into this, produces 40,000+ live hours a year and now runs 24/7 on TikTok Shop.
  Forbes' framing is exact: **the product page becomes a person.**
- **Creators as demand infrastructure.** Food creator content alone draws **420B+ monthly video
  views**; a single Keith Lee–pattern video puts hundreds of customers through a restaurant
  overnight. This is not advertising — it's a human functioning as a distribution channel for a
  physical business.
- **Sales bimodalizes hard.** Inside sales and SDR work is among the most exposed roles in the
  economy; relationship-bound, high-consideration selling is among the least.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **Rev-share creator partnerships** — creators taking attributed demand rather than flat fees,
  which converts influence from a media buy into an ownership stake and makes it a durable
  profession.
- **The vouch as a priced product** — one person's endorsement transferring trust in a single step.
- **Selling to agents** — when the buyer is an AI, someone must make a business legible and
  trustworthy to it, *and* someone must be the human it escalates to.
- **The scene-maker** — the person who makes a place, a neighborhood or a category *matter*. Creates
  enormous value, is almost never compensated directly, and is therefore mispriced.
- **Live selling for services**, not goods. Proven on collectibles, now fastest-growing in beauty
  (+~800%). Nobody has run the format on restaurants, trades or clinics.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Attention futures: forward contracts on a creator's ability to move demand.
- Trust ratings as a tradable asset for individuals.

## Family 14 — COMPETITION AND EXCELLENCE
### *Selling: a human doing something a machine does better, on purpose*

**Force stack:** F9, F7, F8 (3) · **Maturity:** stable and underexploited · **Kill risk:** none obvious

The least intuitive family and, on the evidence, one of the most durable. The premise sounds absurd:
people pay to watch humans do things machines already do better. And yet.

**The chess precedent.** Chess was "solved" as a human contest in 1997 and every forecast said
interest would collapse. Chess is now more popular than at any point in its history, and the money
is in humans playing humans while an engine evaluation bar shows the audience exactly how wrong both
players are. **The machine became the scoreboard for human excellence rather than its replacement.**

**The 2026 confirmation.** MLB introduced the Automated Ball-Strike system as a **challenge** system:
the human umpire calls every pitch, teams may appeal twice a game. League executives chose this over
full automation explicitly because it was more palatable to fans. Given a machine that is measurably
more accurate and a human who is measurably less accurate, a multi-billion-dollar industry chose the
human and demoted the machine to an appeals process. **Accuracy was outsourced; authority was kept
human on purpose.**

**T1 — Obvious:** professional sport, competitive gaming, chess and mind sports, musical competition,
culinary competition.

**T2 — Evolution in flight**
- **Machine as scoreboard, not competitor** — engine analysis, xG, shot quality, ABS. The measure
  makes the human achievement legible and therefore more impressive.
- **Officiating survives as authority** rather than as perception (see above).
- **Participation over spectatorship** — Hyrox, marathons, amateur leagues. If watching is cheap,
  *doing* becomes the premium product.

**T3 — Non-obvious / emerging**
- **"Human-only" certified competition** — verified unassisted and unaugmented, as AI assistance
  becomes undetectable in chess, esports, writing and art prizes. This is Family 9 fused with
  Family 14, and it is coming to every judged competition on earth.
- **Achievement verification** as its own product — see Family 8.
- **Difficulty as entertainment** — formats built around humans attempting what machines find
  trivial, watched *because* of the gap.
- **Amateur excellence markets** — as professional excellence gets machine-benchmarked into
  meaninglessness, local and amateur achievement gains relative value.

**T4 — Speculative**
- Augmentation classes, like weight classes: unassisted / assisted / open divisions, formalized
  across cognitive and physical sport.
- Human record-keeping as a cultural institution — the last unassisted anything.

## Cross-cutting: the multi-family professions

The most defensible positions sit in three or more families at once. This is the practical output of
the whole taxonomy:

| Profession | Families | Why it's hard to dislodge |
|---|---|---|
| **Hospitality / restaurateur** | 1, 5, 7, 8 | Presence + belonging + taste + ritual, sold as one ticket |
| **Nurse** | 2, 4, 10 | Touch + licensure/liability + sustained responsibility |
| **Clergy / celebrant** | 5, 8, 6 | Belonging + witness + transformation; oldest bundle there is |
| **Coach (any kind)** | 6, 5, 2 | Transformation + belonging + often physical presence |
| **Trial lawyer** | 4, 8, 11 | Accountability + witness + advocacy |
| **Skilled trade owner** | 3, 4, 9 | Embodiment + liability + physical verification |
| **Matchmaker** | 5, 7, 8 | Belonging + taste + witness to a life transition |
| **Personal physician** | 2, 4, 6, 10 | Touch + signature + behavior change + continuity |

And the inverse test — the fastest way to check any career or business against this map:

> **If the product can be delivered as a file, it deflates. If it requires a body, a signature, a
> witness, or a relationship, it inflates.**
