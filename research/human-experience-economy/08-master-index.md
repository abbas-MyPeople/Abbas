# Master index — ~280 categories, swept and scored

The widest pass. Everything I could identify that plausibly sits in the human-contact / human-
experience economy, across 24 domains, scored on a common scale. Use this to find things the
narrative documents skipped, and to check any specific profession quickly.

## The scale

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BOOM** | Growing on volume *and* price. Evidence today. |
| **SPLIT** | Bimodalizing — transactional middle hollows out, premium advisory tier and low-cost tier both survive. **The most common outcome in this index.** Per Law 3 in [`07-economic-foundations.md`](07-economic-foundations.md). |
| **PRICE** | Price/status rises, volume flat or falling. Owners capture, workers may not. |
| **VOL** | Volume rises, price suppressed. Jobs, not prosperity. |
| **NEW** | Category didn't meaningfully exist five years ago. |
| **ERODE** | Human share shrinking despite the human-contact framing. Listed for honesty. |
| **?** | Genuinely uncertain; flagged rather than guessed. |

**Fam** = family from [`01-taxonomy.md`](01-taxonomy.md) (1 Presence · 2 Touch · 3 Hands · 4
Accountability · 5 Belonging · 6 Transformation · 7 Taste · 8 Witness · 9 Verification · 10 Care ·
11 Advocacy · 12 Human-as-input · 13 Persuasion · 14 Excellence).

## Reading the index: five things it shows

1. **SPLIT is the modal outcome, not BOOM.** Roughly a third of these bimodalize rather than boom or
   die. The dominant career risk is not being replaced — it's **being stranded in the middle** of a
   profession that survives at both ends. Travel agents, bank tellers and portrait painters all say
   the same thing.

2. **VOL and PRICE almost never co-occur.** Care work is VOL — the most human work in the economy,
   the largest job growth, the worst pay. Concerts are PRICE — enormous revenue growth, flat
   employment. If you want both, you need a supply constraint *and* demand growth, which is rare:
   nursing, licensed trades, and specialist therapy are about the only places it happens.

3. **NEW clusters in three places** — verification (§15), advocacy (§16), and human-as-input (§24).
   All three are *products of* automation rather than survivors of it. If you're looking for
   greenfield, that's where it is.

4. **ERODE shows up inside "human contact" categories.** Tutoring, inside sales, low-acuity therapy,
   commodity journalism, recorded adult content, radiology reads. Human contact was never the moat —
   it was correlated with the moat. Being face-to-face doesn't save you if the work is reproducible.

5. **The strongest single cell in the index is nursing.** Touch + licensure + liability + care +
   demographics + collective bargaining. Six moats. It's also the profession most actively fighting
   for its share of the surplus, and winning — which is why it's the one to watch as the test case
   for whether human-contact workers capture the Baumol premium or merely generate it.
