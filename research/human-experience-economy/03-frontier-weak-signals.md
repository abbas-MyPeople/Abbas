# The frontier — 24 categories that don't have names yet

The taxonomy maps what exists. This document is the speculative edge: professions and businesses
that are either **being born right now under a different name**, or that follow so directly from the
nine forces that their absence is the surprising thing.

Each entry has the same shape:

- **The job** — what the person actually does, with a working title
- **Weak signal** — the observable thing today that implies it
- **Would have to be true** — the falsifiable precondition
- **Clock** — my honest estimate of when it becomes a nameable category
- **Confidence** — high / medium / low, stated so this can be scored later

Sorted roughly by conviction, highest first. The last five are deliberately out on a limb and
labeled as such.

---

## Tier A — high conviction, already forming

### 1. The AI Ombudsman
**The job.** You retain a human, on subscription, to contest automated decisions made about you
anywhere in your life: a denied claim, a credit decline, a platform ban, a benefits termination, a
school placement, an algorithmic price. They know the appeal paths, they have standing, and they
escalate to a human on the other side.

**Weak signal.** Automated denial at scale already spawned an appeals industry. 42% of consumers say
they'd pay for human rep access. EU AI Act Article 14 establishes human oversight as a *right-shaped*
concept. Patient advocates and special-ed advocates already do exactly this in one vertical each.

**Would have to be true.** That the volume of consequential automated decisions per person keeps
rising (near-certain), and that no statutory free-redress regime preempts the market (uncertain —
regulation could either create this profession or nationalize it).

**Clock.** 1–3 years. **Confidence: high.** *This is the single strongest net-new category in the
map.* Every automated decision layer manufactures its own demand for human intercession — it is a
byproduct of automation, not a reaction to it, which means it scales with the thing that's supposed
to be killing human jobs.

---

### 2. The Liability Sponge (professional-as-underwriter)
**The job.** A licensed professional whose product is not the work but the *assumption of
responsibility* for machine-produced work. Insured, credentialed, priced on exposure rather than
hours.

**Weak signal.** EU AI Act Art. 14 and NIST AI RMF both demand *provable* human oversight. Medical
over-read, audit sign-off and PE stamping already work this way. The compliance literature's own
warning — that an untrained human in the loop is "a liability dressed up as process" — is a
product-shaped hole.

**Would have to be true.** That insurers develop a price spread between human-signed and
machine-signed work. **That spread, once it appears, is the whole business model** — watch for it as
the leading indicator.

**Clock.** 2–4 years. **Confidence: high.**

---

### 3. In-person verification as a compliance function
**The job.** Physically confirming that a person, asset or condition is real, because remote
channels became untrustworthy. Not a security guard — a credentialed attester whose signature has
legal weight.

**Weak signal.** Arup lost $25M to a fully synthetic video call. Zoom shipped "Verified Human"
badges. The security literature's own conclusion is that when the verification medium becomes the
attack vector, defense shifts *from perception to process* — and the terminal process is a body in
a room.

**Would have to be true.** That detection keeps losing to generation. Current trend line says yes;
this is an arms race and arms races are not safely extrapolated.

**Clock.** Now–2 years. **Confidence: high.** The under-appreciated second-order effect: **this
quietly reverses a decade of remote-first orthodoxy, and it does so through the compliance
department rather than through anyone's preference about office culture.** Nobody will call it
return-to-office. It'll be called fraud control.

---

### 4. The Escalation Tier (human contact as the paid SKU)
**The job.** Not "customer service." A named, persistent human relationship sold as the premium
product, where the AI tier is free and adequate.

**Weak signal.** Klarna's CEO said the quiet part: AI service is the *cheap* service, and human
connection becomes the VIP experience. >⅔ of consumers already expect this split. Telecoms, banks
and SaaS are shipping it now.

**Would have to be true.** Only that AI service quality keeps improving — which *accelerates* this
rather than threatening it. The better the free tier, the more the paid human tier reads as luxury.

**Clock.** Now. **Confidence: high.** Already happening; still unnamed as a category.

---

### 5. Account and identity recovery specialists
**The job.** You get locked out by an automated system with no human on the other end — bank,
platform, government portal, medical record — and it's a catastrophic life event. This person gets
you back in.

**Weak signal.** Already a common, undignified crisis with no formal profession attached, currently
handled by Reddit threads and knowing someone who works there. Deepfake-driven account takeover
makes it worse in both directions: more lockouts *and* more legitimate reason for institutions to
harden.

**Would have to be true.** That automated identity systems keep producing false negatives at scale.
Certain.

**Clock.** 1–3 years. **Confidence: high.** There is no 911 for this and there should be.

---

### 6. Care coordinator for the family
**The job.** Professional orchestration of an elder's or disabled person's care across aides,
specialists, insurers, pharmacies and AI triage systems. Currently unpaid labor performed by adult
daughters at enormous career cost.

**Weak signal.** 65+ population +12.8M by 2034. ~53M unpaid US caregivers. Care fragmenting into
more providers, more automation, more coordination surface — the opposite of simplification.

**Would have to be true.** That someone other than the exhausted family member pays — employers
(retention of the sandwich generation) or payers (avoided hospitalization). **The buyer is the
question, not the need.**

**Clock.** 2–4 years. **Confidence: high.**

---

## Tier B — medium conviction, clear signal

### 7. Presence attestation ("verified live")
**The job.** Cryptographically and procedurally certifying that an event actually happened, live,
with those humans present. C2PA for moments rather than files.

**Weak signal.** C2PA provenance standards spreading through professional capture; phone-free events
becoming a bookable feature; live-content value rising precisely as recorded content is devalued by
synthesis.

**Would have to be true.** That "was this live?" becomes a question people can't answer from the
artifact. Approximately already true.

**Clock.** 2–4 years. **Confidence: medium.**

---

### 8. The professional host
**The job.** Assembling rooms of strangers who click. Supper clubs, dinner series, curated
gatherings — as a career with a rate card, not a side hustle.

**Weak signal.** Commercial third-spaces growing (Othership, Bathhouse); dinner-party companies;
run-club economics; the entire "IRL" wave. The skill is real and currently uncredentialed.

**Would have to be true.** That people pay for *the room* rather than the food/venue. Partly proven;
margins are the open question.

**Clock.** Now–2 years. **Confidence: medium.** Low barrier to entry means fast commoditization —
the durable version is B2B (see #9).

---

### 9. Belonging as an employee benefit
**The job.** Selling community to employers rather than consumers. Corporate belonging programs,
loneliness-outcome contracting, community as a retention and health-cost lever.

**Weak signal.** Corporate offsites are a $73.7B industry *because of* remote work. Loneliness has
documented health costs. Employers already buy gym memberships and mental-health apps on exactly
this logic.

**Would have to be true.** A measurable link between social connection programs and either health
spend or retention, robust enough for a CFO. Not yet established.

**Clock.** 2–5 years. **Confidence: medium.** **The 10x moment is when a payer reimburses social
connection** — the day a billing code exists for this, the category changes scale overnight. Watch
Medicare Advantage supplemental benefits.

---

### 10. The tacit-knowledge harvester
**The job.** Debriefing masters of physical and social crafts before they retire or die — capturing
what was never written down, for both AI training and human apprenticeship.

**Weak signal.** Egocentric video collection for embodied AI is an active research program. Expert
data marketplaces pay up to ~$200/hr for credentialed judgment. Boomer retirement is removing
irreplaceable process knowledge from manufacturing and the trades right now.

**Would have to be true.** That tacit knowledge stays economically valuable after capture — i.e.
that capturing it doesn't instantly commoditize it. Uncomfortably uncertain; this may be a job whose
success destroys its own market.

**Clock.** Now–3 years. **Confidence: medium.** Real clock pressure: the holders are dying.

---

### 11. Judgment and taste training
**The job.** Teaching people to evaluate machine output — to know when a fluent, confident,
well-formatted answer is wrong. The core professional skill of the era, taught essentially nowhere.

**Weak signal.** Every profession absorbing AI is discovering that review is harder than production.
The "human in the loop without authority to overrule" failure mode is the same problem in
institutional form.

**Would have to be true.** That evaluation stays humanly tractable, i.e. models don't get so good
that human review is net-negative. In some narrow domains that line has already been crossed.

**Clock.** 2–4 years. **Confidence: medium.**

---

### 12. Secular rites of passage
**The job.** Designing and officiating ceremonies for transitions that currently go unmarked:
career change, divorce, recovery milestones, retirement, menopause, coming of age, citizenship.

**Weak signal.** Celebration-of-life services, living funerals, death cafés, secular celebrants — the
ritual demand is visibly surviving the institutional decline that was supposed to kill it. Gen Z
religious attendance rising, notably among young men, points at the same underlying hunger.

**Would have to be true.** That ritual demand is genuinely independent of religious belief. The
funeral evidence says yes.

**Clock.** 3–6 years. **Confidence: medium.**

---

### 13. Meaning counseling for the displaced expert
**The job.** Not career services. Closer to chaplaincy — for people whose *identity* was their
expertise, and whose expertise just got commoditized. Radiologists, translators, illustrators,
paralegals, and eventually many more.

**Weak signal.** The therapist shortage; the specific psychological texture of AI displacement
(it's not "I lost my job," it's "the thing I was is no longer special"); the volume of professional
identity crisis visible in every professional community online.

**Would have to be true.** That displacement is broad enough to be a market rather than a footnote.
Genuinely contested — see [`04-counterarguments.md`](04-counterarguments.md).

**Clock.** 2–5 years. **Confidence: medium.** Emotionally the most important entry in this document.

---

### 14. Anti-slop curation as paid infrastructure
**The job.** Certifying "a human read this and it's worth your time," at the level of a whole
category rather than one newsletter.

**Weak signal.** Search results filling with generated content; the human-made premium; 67% of
consumers wanting AI content labeled; the observable retreat of discovery from search toward trusted
individuals.

**Would have to be true.** That the volume of generated content overwhelms filtering models —
i.e. that AI can't solve a problem AI created. Plausible for taste, not for spam.

**Clock.** 1–3 years. **Confidence: medium.**

---

### 15. Reputation defense against synthetic content
**The job.** Defending real people and businesses against AI-generated defamation, impersonation,
fake reviews and voice cloning. Part legal, part technical, entirely human-fronted.

**Weak signal.** Deepfake volume up sharply since 2024; executive protection firms extending from
bodies to likenesses; small businesses already being extorted with fabricated reviews.

**Would have to be true.** That takedown and detection remain slow and adversarial. Currently true.

**Clock.** Now–2 years. **Confidence: medium.**

---

### 16. Attention rehabilitation
**The job.** Treating fractured attention as a trainable, possibly clinical deficit — not as a
wellness retreat.

**Weak signal.** Digital detox retreats as a commercial category; phone-free venues; Yondr in schools
and weddings; the Offline Club and grassroots detox movements; the AI-companion deskilling research.

**Would have to be true.** That attention damage is real, measurable and remediable. Contested
science; commercially it's already selling regardless.

**Clock.** 3–6 years. **Confidence: medium.** Medicalization is the phase change to watch.

---

### 17. The robot wrangler
**The job.** Field technician for deployed robot fleets — exceptions, recovery, maintenance, and the
last 5% of dexterity no policy handles.

**Weak signal.** Teleoperation throughput of 5–50 episodes/hour is the binding constraint on
embodied AI; every real robot deployment so far quietly runs on human exception-handling.

**Would have to be true.** That robots deploy widely *and* imperfectly — the most likely world by a
wide margin.

**Clock.** 2–5 years. **Confidence: medium.** The rare job that scales *with* automation, which
makes it strategically interesting for anyone hedging.

---

### 18. Intergenerational brokerage
**The job.** Deliberately connecting age cohorts — elders with young people — for care, mentorship,
housing and companionship. Both groups are lonely, and neither is served.

**Weak signal.** The demographic wave (+12.8M elders by 2034), Gen Z loneliness at ~67%, and the
complete absence of institutions that mix ages now that church, union and neighborhood have thinned.

**Would have to be true.** That someone pays. Likely a payer or a housing operator, not the
participants.

**Clock.** 3–6 years. **Confidence: medium.**

---

## Tier C — low conviction, high interest

### 19. The scarcity-engineered experience
**The job.** Producing deliberately unrecordable, unrepeatable events sold at a premium *because*
they leave no artifact. The anti-content business model: you had to be there, and there is no proof.

**Weak signal.** Phone-free events exploding; Q1 2026 alone exceeded a third of all 2025 volume;
79% of young adults valuing spontaneity at events.

**Would have to be true.** That people pay *more* for less shareability — which inverts a decade of
event marketing economics. Early evidence says the experience improves; pricing power is unproven.

**Clock.** 2–5 years. **Confidence: low-medium.**

---

### 20. Human disagreement as a product
**The job.** Supplying structured expert disagreement to AI labs — because the places where
credentialed humans genuinely diverge are exactly where models have no signal and cannot manufacture
any.

**Weak signal.** Expert data marketplaces already pay premium rates for specialized judgment;
frontier evaluation increasingly needs humans who can adjudicate superhuman output.

**Would have to be true.** That labs value disagreement rather than treating it as noise to be
averaged out. Genuinely uncertain — current practice mostly averages it away.

**Clock.** 2–4 years. **Confidence: low.**

---

### 21. The witness network
**The job.** An on-call human attestation layer — the physical-world analogue of a certificate
authority. Need a verified human to confirm something in Cleveland in two hours? Dispatch.

**Weak signal.** Notarization, inspection, appraisal and proof-of-life all exist as fragmented
one-vertical versions. The horizontal doesn't.

**Would have to be true.** That legal frameworks recognize distributed human attestation. Slow-moving
and jurisdictional.

**Clock.** 4–8 years. **Confidence: low.**

---

### 22. Personal judgment licensing
**The job.** Individuals licensing their preference function — a model trained on your taste, with
royalties flowing to you.

**Weak signal.** Expert data marketplaces are the crude precursor (renting judgment by the hour
rather than licensing it as an asset). Voice and likeness licensing already exists for performers.

**Would have to be true.** Working provenance and attribution for model contributions, which is
technically unsolved and legally contested.

**Clock.** 5–10 years. **Confidence: low.** The bull case is enormous: it converts human
distinctiveness into a rent-yielding asset.

---

### 23. AI-era mourning
**The job.** Ritual and grief support for losses that didn't previously exist — an obsoleted
profession, a digital afterlife, a discontinued model that someone talked to daily for three years.

**Weak signal.** The intensity of public reaction to model deprecations is already anomalous and
publicly observable. Character.AI has ~20M MAU, over half under 24. Grief support is a growing
market on the conventional side.

**Would have to be true.** That these attachments are real enough to require real grief work. The
early clinical literature suggests they are, and the lawsuits suggest the stakes are not theoretical.

**Clock.** 3–7 years. **Confidence: low.** Sounds absurd. So did death doulas in 2012.

---

### 24. Analog assurance
**The job.** Deliberately non-networked, human-operated systems sold on unhackability — paper
ballots, mechanical backups, air-gapped records, physical originals — staffed and audited by people.

**Weak signal.** Physical original custody re-emerging as ground truth where digital provenance is
contestable; the return of in-person proctoring after remote assessment was defeated.

**Would have to be true.** A significant trust failure in digital systems, large enough to move
institutional policy. One bad election or one major provenance scandal away.

**Clock.** 3–8 years. **Confidence: low.** Note that this is a *reversion* category — the pattern
where AI resurrects a dead industry rather than creating a new one. In-person proctoring already did
this. Expect more.

## The four patterns underneath all 24

Worth extracting, because these generalize beyond the specific bets:

1. **Automation manufactures its own human demand.** The AI ombudsman, escalation tiers, identity
   recovery, robot wranglers, liability sponges — none of these are resistance to automation. They
   are *produced by* it, and they scale with it. If you're looking for the most robust categories,
   look for jobs whose demand curve is a function of automation volume.

2. **Adversarial dynamics protect humans permanently.** Where the problem is another intelligence
   trying to deceive you — fraud, impersonation, defamation, evaluation of a system optimizing to
   look correct — there is no stable automated equilibrium. Someone has to be accountable at the end
   of the chain. These are the least perishable categories in the entire map.

3. **AI resurrects dead industries.** In-person proctoring. The reference check. Physical originals.
   Paper. The witnessed signature. When digital shortcuts get defeated, the thing they replaced comes
   back — repriced, and now with better margins. A systematically under-searched place to look for
   opportunity: *what did the internet kill that only worked because forgery was hard?*

4. **The bridge jobs are paid to burn themselves.** Teleoperation and tacit-knowledge harvesting pay
   well precisely because they're the input to their own obsolescence. That's not a reason to avoid
   them — the pay is real and it's now — but it's a reason to treat them as a capital-accumulation
   phase, never as a career.
