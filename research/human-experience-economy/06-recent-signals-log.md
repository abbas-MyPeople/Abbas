# Recent signals log — what's happened in the last ~120 days

*Maintained as of **2026-07-28**. Newest and most load-bearing first. This is the document that
should get stale fastest — if it hasn't been touched in a quarter, distrust the rest of the folder.*

The thesis in this folder is a moving target and the ground shifted meaningfully between roughly
April and July 2026. Five things changed that a January-2026 version of this research would have
gotten wrong.

---

## The five things that changed this quarter

### 1. The entry-level rung broke, and the middle didn't

This is the single most important labor development of 2026 and it reframes the whole thesis.

- Stanford finds a **13–16% relative decline in entry-level hiring for 22–25 year olds** in
  AI-exposed occupations. UK government data shows exposed job adverts down **38%**. Entry-level
  postings down ~15% YoY.
- **For the first time on record, AI is the leading stated reason US companies give for layoffs.**
- And yet: three and a half years after ChatGPT, aggregate labor data on both sides of the Atlantic
  show **no economy-wide AI jobs shock**. The softening is broad and cyclical; the genuine AI effect
  is narrow and concentrated at the bottom rung of the most exposed occupations — software,
  customer support — precisely where output is cheaply verifiable.
- The mutation, and this is the striking part: junior roles in highly exposed jobs are **seven times
  more likely to demand senior-level skills** — judgment, leadership. These "seniorized" entry-level
  roles have **grown 35% since 2019** while other entry-level roles declined 10%.

([Forbes, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ronschmelzer/2026/07/14/ai-isnt-taking-every-job-its-targeting-these-workers/);
[Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/ai-isnt-coming-for-your-job-yet-and-maybe-never-will/))

**Why it matters here.** It confirms the thesis's *shape* and destroys its comfortable version.
What's being destroyed is the **apprenticeship function of the economy** — the rungs where humans
used to acquire the judgment that the thesis says is now precious. Every family in the taxonomy that
depends on seasoned human judgment (Family 4, 7, 11) has a supply problem arriving in about ten
years, because we are currently not manufacturing juniors.

**Add this as a tenth force: F10 — the judgment supply crisis.** Demand for experienced human
judgment rises while the pipeline that produces it is being dismantled. That is a *scarcity
amplifier*, and it makes the human premium worse — and more unequal — not better.

### 2. Klarna finished the round trip, and it became the canonical case

Klarna replaced ~700 customer service agents with AI, then rehired humans. The CEO: *"We focused too
much on efficiency and cost. The result was lower quality, and that's not sustainable."* And more
pointedly: *"there will always be a human if you want."*

By mid-2026 this is **the** enterprise cautionary tale — executives pitching AI workforce plans are
now routinely asked to explain how they avoid the Klarna outcome. The settled model is hybrid: AI
takes tier-one volume, humans take escalation, judgment, emotion and relationship.

([CX Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/);
[Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396))

**Why it matters here.** The same CEO also said AI service will be the *cheap* service and human
connection the VIP tier. Both statements are true and they define the shape of Family 11: **the
human doesn't come back as the default — the human comes back as the escalation tier and the
premium tier.** Fewer humans, higher stakes per human, priced up. That's not a restoration, it's a
restratification.

### 3. Robots: real deployments, but the humanoid narrative outran the hardware

- **Figure AI has surpassed 10,000 deployments** across partner warehouses; BMW Spartanburg moved
  from trial to active parts handling.
- **Tesla Optimus production at Fremont had not started as of mid-July 2026** (guidance: late July/
  August). Musk said on the Q4 2025 call that Optimus was *"not in usage in our factories in a
  material way"* and units were *"primarily for learning, not productive tasks."* Widely circulated
  cumulative unit figures do not come from Tesla, which has never published a production count.
  No consumer sales, pre-orders or waitlist; consumer availability targeted end-2027 at earliest.
- **1X opened pre-orders for NEO**, a consumer home humanoid, with 2026 delivery claimed.
- Industry-wide, July 2026 is the moment the field split into *verified deployments in live
  industrial environments* versus *well-funded prototype labs still in "coming soon."*

([Technology.org, 18 Jul 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/07/18/humanoid-robots-in-2026-what-is-actually-deployed/);
[deployment tracker, Jul 2026](https://humanoidapplications.com/humanoid-robot-deployment-report-real-world-milestones-industry-adoption-tracker-july-2026/))

**Why it matters here.** The embodiment gap (F4) is holding for unstructured environments and
closing in structured ones — exactly as predicted, but the *warehouse* end is moving faster than
expected and the *home* end slower. Families 2, 3 and 10 are safe for now in homes, hospitals and
job sites; anything that looks like a warehouse task should be discounted hard.

### 4. Agentic commerce crossed from demo to measurable share

- AI platforms accounted for **$20.9B in retail spending in 2026 — 1.5% of total retail, nearly
  quadrupling 2025**.
- **73% of consumers already use AI somewhere in the shopping journey**; 70% are at least somewhat
  comfortable with an agent purchasing on their behalf; but only 13% have completed a purchase
  after an AI referral.
- Morgan Stanley projects **~half of online shoppers using AI shopping agents by 2030**, at ~25% of
  their spend.
- The disintermediation story has already been revised once: early data killed the "ChatGPT owns
  discovery-to-checkout" narrative, and the emerging picture is agents as a *new intermediary layer*
  rather than a replacement.

([commercetools](https://commercetools.com/blog/the-agentic-commerce-radar-key-market-shifts-insights);
[Berkeley CMR, Apr 2026](https://cmr.berkeley.edu/2026/04/the-rise-of-ai-intermediaries-how-agentic-systems-are-rewiring-customer-relationships/))

**Why it matters here.** This is the counter-current to Family 7 (taste and curation). Machine
curation is scaling fast. But note what's happening simultaneously in §5 below — **human-hosted
selling is growing faster than agentic selling**, off a much bigger base. Both are eating the same
meal: the middle, where discovery was neither personal nor automated. Undifferentiated search,
generic listings, and the unstaffed shelf are what's dying.

### 5. Live human selling turned out to be a bigger business than AI selling

Genuinely under-covered, and it forced a new family into the taxonomy:

- **US live commerce reached $42B in 2026, growing at 36% CAGR** through 2028. US livestream
  ecommerce grew ~50% in 2025 alone to $14.64B.
- **TikTok Live shopping converts at 7.8% versus 2.1% for feed ads — 3.7x.** Live's share of US
  TikTok Shop GMV went from 14% (2024) to 26%.
- QVC — 40 years into live human selling — now produces **40,000+ hours a year** of live shoppable
  content and launched a 24/7 US live channel on TikTok Shop.

([QVC](https://www.qvcgrp.com/newsroom/pressrelease/qvc-group-launches-first-u-s-24-7-live-social-shopping-experience-with-tiktok-shop/);
[eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/insights/livestreaming-trends-stats))

**Why it matters here.** A person, live, talking to you converts nearly 4x better than an
algorithmically optimized ad. That is the human premium showing up as a hard conversion metric
rather than a survey preference — the strongest quantitative evidence in this entire folder, because
nobody is *asked* anything; they just buy more. This is now **Family 13 — Persuasion and Trust
Transfer** in [`01-taxonomy.md`](01-taxonomy.md).

## Everything else, by family

### Family 1 — Presence
- **82% of event attendees now prefer in-person; 1% prefer virtual** — up from 67.8% preferring
  in-person in 2023. That's a dramatic three-year swing.
- **84% of consumer marketers and 86% of B2B marketers plan to increase event spending in 2026.**
  Experiential campaigns reportedly return 3:1 to 5:1.
- 65% of top-searched travel dates for 2026 align with live events — fandom is now a primary
  long-haul travel motivator.
- Experience economy sizing (C-grade, definitions vary wildly): >$1T in 2026, $2.1T by 2032.

### Family 5 — Belonging
- **Gallup: the share of US adults who drink alcohol has fallen to 54% — the lowest in nearly 90
  years.** 44% of Gen Z actively seeks sober-friendly nightlife; non-alcoholic consumption expected
  up a third in 2026 alone. **The third place is being rebuilt without its historical solvent**,
  which is a once-a-century change in how social infrastructure works. Sober bars, run clubs and
  bathhouses are all answers to the same question.
- **Camino de Santiago: 530,000+ completions in 2025, on track for a record 2026** — more than 10x
  the 55,004 of the year 2000. Fátima visits +26.5% YoY. Pilgrimage is a global growth category and
  it is only partly religious.
- Creator IRL: VidCon's 15th anniversary; a dense mid-2026 calendar of ticketed creator events;
  fan sweet-spot ticket price $10–50, with >1 in 3 attendees very likely to purchase at the event.

### Family 2 / 10 — Touch and Care
- **Nurse strikes have nearly quadrupled since 2017.** 16 healthcare strikes in the first half of
  2026. 31,000 UNAC/UHCP nurses won **21.5% across-the-board wage increases** from Kaiser after a
  month-long walkout; NYC saw its largest-ever nurses' strike at ~15,000. Oregon's SB 916 lets
  striking workers claim unemployment, structurally weakening the "starve them out" tactic.
  → **This is the F1/Baumol prediction arriving as labor militancy rather than as market pricing.**
  Watch this closely: it's the mechanism by which care workers might actually capture the surplus,
  and it's the answer to the "volume boom without prosperity" objection in
  [`04-counterarguments.md`](04-counterarguments.md) §1.
- **Autism therapy:** CDC now estimates 1 in 36 children; **132,000 open BCBA positions against
  ~74,300 active BCBAs.** The sector reframed its problem in 2026 from "shortage" to "workforce
  durability."
- **Peer support specialists are the fastest-growing role in behavioral health**, driven by CMS
  recognition of peer support as a **billable Medicaid service**.
  → **This falsifies my own "watch for it" prediction in Family 5 by proving it already happened.**
  A billing code for human connection *exists*. The prediction should be upgraded from "watch for"
  to "extend": the question is no longer whether payers will reimburse lived-experience human
  support, but how far the precedent travels.
- **988 / crisis:** ~76,255 crisis counselors nationally; 89% of 988 centers report difficulty
  funding hiring, 87% can't staff to call volume. The provider network meets **26% of actual need.**

### Family 6 — Transformation
- **Psilocybin facilitation is now a real licensed profession**: Oregon lists **114 licensed
  facilitators and 24 service centers**; Colorado has 35 healing centers and distinguishes clinical
  from non-clinical "original" facilitators; New Mexico's medical program arrives late 2026. The AMA
  has issued **Category III CPT codes (0820T–0823T)**, upgradeable to reimbursable Category I as
  early as 2026–27.
  → **A brand-new, state-licensed, human-only profession created from scratch this decade, whose
  entire job description is "sit with a person for eight hours."** The single best existence proof
  in this folder that new irreducibly-human professions get invented, not just preserved.

### Family 3 — Hands on the world
- Hospitality is heading into 2026 with an **18% projected labor shortfall**, 70–80% annual turnover
  (~5x other industries), and US hotels paying **$131B in wages and benefits**, up 15.3% on 2019
  against revenue up only 12.8%. Baumol, arriving on a P&L.
- **42% of restaurant operators were unprofitable last year; >9 in 10 flag labor as a significant
  challenge.**

### Family 8 — Witness and ritual
- **MLB's Automated Ball-Strike system debuted in 2026 — as a *challenge* system.** The human umpire
  makes every call; teams may appeal two per game. League executives chose it over full automation
  as the more palatable option *for fans*, and the consensus is that it works and improves the game.
  → **The most elegant emblem in this entire folder.** Given a choice between a machine that is
  measurably more accurate and a human who is measurably less accurate, a multi-billion-dollar
  industry chose *the human, with the machine as an appeals process.* That is Family 4 and Family 8
  in a single rule change: authority stays human, accuracy is outsourced, and the human remains
  because the human is the point.

### Family 9 — Trust and verification
- **~52% of new articles published online are AI-generated** (though mostly low-quality filler);
  ~51% of web traffic is bots, 37% "bad bots."
- The counter-fact that matters: **only 14–17% of Google's top results are AI-generated; quality
  search content remains ~82.7% human-made.** So the slop is real, and the *filters still favor
  humans.* Family 7 (anti-slop curation) is supported by both numbers at once — the flood is real
  and the premium for filtering it is real.

### Family 7 / 8 — the analog reversion
- **Vinyl grew for a 19th consecutive year**: 47.9M units, +8.6%, with >40% sold through independent
  record stores.
- **Independent bookstores up ~70% since 2020**; Barnes & Noble opened ~70 stores in 2025 with ~60
  more planned for 2026.
- Fortune (14 Jul 2026): Gen Z's analog obsession is reviving the **film camera market that digital
  killed**. Polaroid instant camera purchases +15%.
  → The pattern from [`03-frontier-weak-signals.md`](03-frontier-weak-signals.md) — *AI resurrects
  dead industries* — is now visible in consumer hardware, not just in proctoring and notarization.

### The luxury human — new hard numbers
- **Private chefs now earn up to $300,000; butlers up to $180,000; multilingual traveling nannies up
  to $163,000.** Recruiters describe the busiest market for estate and domestic staff **in 20 years.**
  ([CNBC, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/10/private-chef-salaries.html))
  → The status premium (F7) with a wage number attached. Also the clearest illustration of the
  affordability objection: this is the human premium accruing entirely to households that can pay
  $300K for a cook.

## What I'd change in the rest of the folder based on this quarter

1. **Add F10 — the judgment supply crisis** to the forces. The apprenticeship rung is breaking. This
   raises the human premium *and* makes it more unequal, and it has a ten-year fuse.
2. **Add Family 13 — Persuasion and Trust Transfer.** Live commerce at $42B and 3.7x conversion is
   too large and too well-measured to leave inside "taste."
3. **Add Family 14 — Competition and Excellence.** The ABS decision, chess's post-engine boom and
   human-only leagues point at a category where the human doing it *is* the product, independent of
   performance.
4. **Upgrade the payer-reimbursement prediction from speculative to in-progress.** Medicaid already
   reimburses peer support; the AMA already has codes for psychedelic facilitation. The billing code
   for human presence exists.
5. **Downgrade near-term humanoid risk for homes and hospitals; upgrade it for warehouses.** Figure's
   10,000 deployments and Optimus's non-start are pulling in opposite directions and both are real.
6. **Add the Klarna round trip as the reference case** for how human work returns: as an escalation
   and premium tier, not as a restoration of headcount.
